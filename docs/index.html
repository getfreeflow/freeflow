Hold a key. Say the thing. Send it without editing.

FreeFlow listens while you hold a key, works out what you said on your own Mac, and drops finished text wherever your cursor already was. No account. Nothing uploaded. No monthly bill.

01 / the gap

You already know
talking is faster.

Roughly three times faster. That has been true the whole time.

The reason you still type is that raw speech-to-text hands you something you would never send to another person. No punctuation. Every um kept faithfully. Sentences that restart halfway through and never finish.

The distance between what you said and what you would actually send is the entire product.

  • Dictate. Read it back. Fix the punctuation.
  • Delete the false start.
  • Rewrite the middle sentence.
  • Wonder why you did not just type it.

03 / everything in the box

Nine things, one app.

Dictation

Hold, talk, release. Text lands at the cursor. Tap instead of holding and it latches on until you say otherwise.

Commands

Select anything, hold the command key, say what you want done. "Cut this in half." The rewrite replaces the selection.

Meetings

Records both halves of a call off your Mac's own audio. Nothing joins the call. Nobody gets told a bot arrived.

Style

A line in Slack and a paragraph in Pages should not come out sounding the same. Per-app rules, all editable.

Vocabulary

The words it keeps mangling. Your name, your colleagues, the product nobody outside the company has heard of.

Snippets

Say two words, insert a paragraph. Expanded before cleanup runs, so the result still gets shaped for where it is going.

Scratchpad

A page that is always open, for the thought that does not have anywhere to live yet.

Insights

How much you actually said, how fast you say it, and which apps it all went into.

Your key, your call

fn by default, because nothing else wants it. Right Option and Right Control work too. Chords are ignored, so ⌘C still copies.

04 / it never leaves

Your voice does not
go anywhere.

The speech model runs on your Mac. Not on a server that belongs to somebody else. There is no account to make, because there is nothing to log in to.

edge of your Mac

Turn cleanup off and it is fully offline

One switch in Settings and the app makes no network requests whatsoever. Pull the wifi and dictation keeps working.

You can read every line of it

All of it is on GitHub under MIT. There are no credentials in the codebase, which is what makes it safe to fork.

Cleanup uses a key you own

If you want the polish pass, you bring your own Groq key. Free tier, no card. Skip it and you get the transcript as spoken.

06 / straight answers

Things you
were going
to ask.

Is it actually free, or free for now?

Free. MIT licensed, no paid tier planned, nothing to upgrade to. The one thing that can cost money is the optional cleanup pass, and that runs on Groq's free tier, which does not ask for a card.

Does my voice get uploaded?

No. Transcription happens on your Mac with a Whisper model you download once. If you enable cleanup, the text of what you said goes to Groq to be tidied. Turn cleanup off and nothing leaves the machine at all.

Why is there no app to download?

Distributing a Mac app that opens without a Gatekeeper warning requires the Apple Developer Program at $99 a year. This is a free project. Building from source sidesteps it entirely and takes about ten minutes.

How is this different from the dictation already in macOS?

Apple's built-in dictation transcribes. It does not punctuate well, does not remove your filler words, does not know your colleagues' names, and does not write differently in Slack than in Mail. That editing pass is the point.

Will it fight with my existing shortcuts?

It shouldn't. The event tap observes keys without consuming them, and a trigger only counts once it has been held alone. Press ⌘C and it stays out of the way.

Does it work in Electron apps?

Yes. It tries a direct accessibility write first, which leaves your clipboard untouched, and falls back to clipboard and a synthetic ⌘V, which works essentially everywhere including Slack, Discord and VS Code.
